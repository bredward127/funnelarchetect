The 27-Point Marketing Checklist

Run this top to bottom before any campaign goes live. Start with Tracking — it is the shortest section and catches the most expensive mistakes.

Tracking & Attribution

  1. Analytics fires on every page. Load each page and confirm a pageview in real-time reporting.
  2. Conversion event fires once. Submit a test lead and confirm exactly one event — not zero, not two.
  3. UTM parameters on every inbound link. Source, medium, and campaign at minimum.
  4. UTMs survive the redirect. If traffic passes through a shortener, confirm parameters arrive intact.
  5. Ad platform pixel installed and verified. Use the platform's own debug tool, not just "it's in the code."
  6. Thank-you page is excluded from ad targeting. Otherwise you pay to retarget people who already converted.

Mobile

  1. Test on a real phone. Browser devtools miss touch and keyboard behavior.
  2. Tap targets are at least 44×44px. Buttons, links, form fields.
  3. The email keyboard appears. type="email" and autocomplete="email" on the input.
  4. No horizontal scroll at 320px width. The narrowest common viewport.
  5. The CTA is visible without scrolling. On a short screen, above the fold still matters.

Copy & Offer

  1. The headline names a specific result. Not a category — a result.
  2. One call to action, repeated. Competing CTAs split attention and lower conversion.
  3. The CTA button says what happens next. "Send Me the Checklist" beats "Submit."
  4. Benefits are outcomes, not features. What changes for them, not what it contains.
  5. The top objection is answered on the page. Price, time, difficulty, or trust.
  6. Social proof is specific. A number or a named quote, not "loved by thousands."
  7. No navigation menu on the landing page. Every extra link is an exit.
  8. Privacy line sits directly under the form. Reduces hesitation at the exact moment of doubt.

Page Speed

  1. Largest Contentful Paint under 2.5s. Measure on a throttled 4G profile, not your office wifi.
  2. Cumulative Layout Shift under 0.1. Set explicit width and height on every image.
  3. Images are compressed and correctly sized. Serve WebP or AVIF where you can.
  4. Fonts are preloaded or swapped. Invisible text during load costs conversions.
  5. No render-blocking scripts in the head. Defer anything that is not critical.

Follow-Up

  1. The welcome email actually sends. Test with a real address on a provider you do not control.
  2. It does not land in spam or Promotions. Check Gmail, Outlook, and one more.
  3. The delivery link works from inside the email. Test the link, not just the page it should reach.
